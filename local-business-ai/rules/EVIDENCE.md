# Evidence & Findings Framework

This rule dictates how evidence is evaluated, structured, and presented as findings.

## The Serious Problem Framework
A serious problem should generally have:
- High business impact
- Strong evidence
- Actionability/fixability
- AND (a Meaningful Competitive Gap OR an Intrinsic Business Risk/Weakness)

## The FACT → INTERPRETATION → RECOMMENDATION Framework
Every important finding must follow this structure:
1. **FACT**: What was actually observed (raw data, verified).
2. **INTERPRETATION**: What that evidence reasonably means for the business.
3. **RECOMMENDATION**: What should be done to resolve it.

## Strict Prohibition on Unsupported Claims
- Do NOT make unsupported claims (e.g., Guaranteed ranking improvements, Exact revenue loss caused by an SEO issue, Exact percentage ranking increases).
- Do NOT claim that a specific fix guarantees a particular Google position.
- Do NOT create fake precision.
- Do NOT claim every missing directory automatically causes a specific Google ranking loss. Describe missing listings as citation/local visibility opportunities unless stronger evidence supports a more specific claim.

## Genuine Strengths
The system must also identify genuine strengths. If a business is doing something well, say so. Do not manufacture problems simply because the system needs something to sell.

## Business Service Priority Alignment
The business services are currently prioritized as:
1. Citation Building / Cleanup
2. Google Business Profile Optimization
3. Review Generation
4. Website Development
5. Website Schema Fix
6. Local SEO

**Rule:** The system must NEVER manufacture a problem simply to sell one of these services.
**Correct sequence:** Research → Evidence → Finding → Priority → Recommendation → Relevant Service
**Prohibited sequence:** Service → Find a problem to justify the service.
